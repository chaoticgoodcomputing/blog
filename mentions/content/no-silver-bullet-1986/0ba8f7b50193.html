Is AI a Silver Bullet?

Spencer Elkington 2026-11-06

But, as we look to the horizon of a decade hence, we see no silver bullet

I’m not certain about the range of 1986-1996, but I can at least safely say that we’ve safely hit a couple orders of magnitude of improvement in the last 40 years, even excluding LLMs. Designer/developer Martin Keary, in a recent video about the design and development of Notefinder, mentions that the lead time for an idea entering production can be as low as a few hours. In certain contexts, I notice the same thing: it obviously depends on the urgency of the change, but in a best-case scenario professionally, I’ve seen ideas go from realization -> ideation -> implementation -> review -> release in a day’s time.

Of course, the claim I’ve seen is that agents are another order of magnitude, of which the claim is possibly less certain. While I do think that code implementation is substantially faster, speeding up one part of a process does not mean speeding up the whole process by the same degree. Engineer Michael Nygard writes about this in AI versus Throughput.

The way I’ve put it, colloquially, is that in a situation where you’re at a physical assembly line and a machine in the middle speeds up by 1000%, you’re not looking at a 1000% increase in productivity across the assembly line. You’re looking at the machine before not moving fast enough, and the machine after catching on fire. In the SDLC, this means that design and ideation aren’t moving fast enough, and that the review process has largely caught on fire.
