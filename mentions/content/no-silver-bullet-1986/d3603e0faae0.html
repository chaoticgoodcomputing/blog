No Silver Bullet: Essence and Accident in Software Engineering

Spencer Elkington 2026-11-06

Software is invisible and unvisualizable.

Again with the absolutes here, Brooks!

I think “unvisualizable” concedes defeat before the fight has really started out. I’d once again summon up boundary objects. My personal definition of a good boundary object (without rambling too far) is that a good boundary object is one that gives you the working knowledge you need to do a task.

A recent video about Britain’s National Energy System Operator is a pretty cool example of visualizations of advanced systems. Ultimately, their main dashboard is not a visualization meant to give a full understanding of the energy grid: it’s to get an immediate working knowledge of any issues worth addressing.

I would accept the claim that software is of a high dimensionality, but that makes it difficult to visualize – not impossible. The analogy I’d pull up is principal component analysis, or PCA. For a given system, there may be some “most significant” visualization, or boundary object, that portrays the most essential axis of the software. For Flowthru, that is the DAG. The next visualization might be the next most significant orthogonal projection. Thinking again about Flowthru, this would/could be:

  1. Steps and catalog configurations themselves;
  2. Storage mediums (S3, local system, etc.)

Ultimately, it’s just making sure you understand what a developer’s necessary working knowledge would be, and breaking it down into the necessary visualizations (or other exploratory tools) to drill down to that level (and just that level) in order to get that working knowledge.
